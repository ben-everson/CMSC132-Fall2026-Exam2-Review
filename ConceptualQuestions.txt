Working with different Data Structures.For each scenario below
pick one of the data structures we have covered so far- Lists, Stacks
and Trees- which best suits the needs of the person in the scenario. 
Then explain why you made your choice instead of picking the other data
structures. Include the efficiency of the operations described in each
situation.

Adam is hosting a dinner party for all of his many friends and wants to 
get ready before they arrive. He sets out plates, napkins and silverware 
so that when guests start arriving they can grab what they need and start
helping themselves to Adam's delicious cooking. 

Baal is planting an apple orchard in his backyard. He decides to plant all
of his apple trees in one big line instead of a more senseable arrangement
because he has not yet learned about maps in his Computer science courses
and wants to use a data structure he is more familiar with to store
information on his trees. He may want to go back to his records and record 
that he watered a given tree or record how many apples it produced, but he 
will rarely need to add more trees to his records. 

Claudia is an intrepid student in CMSC131 who is trying to organize her
notes about all of the Java library classes. Remember that all classes 
are subclasses of the Object class. A class has exactly one direct
superclass, but any number of subclasses. She may want to later add notes
for a new class she is learning about without reorganizing her old notes. 
And of course she wants to routinely look through her notes to refresh her 
knowledge on certain topics.
